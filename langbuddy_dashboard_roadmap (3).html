LangBuddy — Pilot Dashboard & Product Roadmap

16-school secondary pilot, Feb–Mar 2026  ·  Prepared Apr 2026

The ambition
LangBuddy transforms how MTL speaking practice happens — not just a better app, but a structural shift in how curriculum time is used.
Vision: trailblazing how tech empowers students to learn with joy, agency and purpose — a model that, once proven in MTL, can reshape how other subjects are taught.
Student
Learns MTL in school but speaks English at home. Needs more chances to practise — with no judgement, low-stakes, no consequence for getting it wrong.
LangBuddy
Opens it, picks a situation, and speaks. Over time, fluency improves and confidence grows. Learning happens with joy, agency and purpose — not obligation.
Teacher
30–40 students, 50 minutes class time. LangBuddy takes on the repetitive practice load — she gets time back to do what only a human teacher can do.
Scale ambition: 420K students. Every student with a safer space to practise. Every teacher with more room to teach.
What the pilot proved — and what we still need to show
Proven by pilot data
Students will use it when prompted. 1,152 students across 16 schools activated during the 8-week pilot.
Speaking practice is happening. 17,056 minutes of spoken practice generated outside the classroom.
Most students spoke at least once. 92% activation rate with low technical failure signals.
Students show goal-setting behaviour. 923 of 1,152 selected at least one learning goal.
Some schools show very strong retention. 3 schools above 85% teacher-prompted return rate.
Organic after-school usage exists. 12.1% used LangBuddy after school at least once with zero self-direction features built.
Still needs to be shown
Does practice improve speaking outcomes? No pre/post assessment data yet — usage ≠ learning. Being addressed by the efficacy research already in motion.
Does LangBuddy free up teacher time? No data yet on whether teachers restructured their lessons.
Can the high-retention school model be replicated? Top school deployment approach needs documentation before 132-school rollout.
Does the existing app work for upper primary without adaptation? Small supervised in-classroom pilot with 3–5 schools in Term 4 — learning before committing to development.
After-school habit is not yet formed. 12.1% used LangBuddy after school at least once — sustained voluntary return is the key challenge ahead.
Why do students not self-direct more? Requires qual research on motivation, product pull, and habit triggers.

Session turn distribution
32% of all sessions had 21+ turns — median 36 turns. When students engage, they go deep.
Finding: The distribution is bimodal — students either bounce at turn 1 (25.6%) or have a sustained conversation (32% with 21+ turns). The challenge is getting more students past the initial threshold, not making deep sessions longer. Fun Topics drives the deepest sessions — 643 of 1,590 high-turn sessions came from the Fun Topics programme (student-chosen, not curriculum-assigned). 🐾 关爱动物 is the single most engaging topic.
Problem statement & north star
Problem statement
MTL students don't get enough speaking practice
With 30–40 students and 50 minutes, teachers can only give each student a fraction of the practice time needed to build fluency. And when students do practise in class, it's in front of peers — high-stakes, anxiety-inducing, and not conducive to risk-taking. LangBuddy gives every student unlimited low-stakes speaking practice, initiated by teachers and sustained by students — making it possible to practise more often, more freely, and more independently than classroom time alone can ever allow.
North star metric
Average sessions completed per active student per week
Measures whether students are practising frequently enough to build fluency — not just whether they activated once.

Upper primary: exploratory pilot, Term 4 (Sep–Nov 2026)
Term 4 — Sep
3–5 schools — supervised in-classroom pilot
A small, tightly supervised in-classroom pilot using the existing app. The research question is "what would we need to adapt?" — not "does it work at scale?". Teacher-supervised, in-class only, focused on learning before committing to product adaptation investment.
Entry criteria: school and teacher champions identified, research questions defined, clear expectation-setting with schools that this is exploratory on the existing secondary product.
Key risk: The high-retention playbook and teacher dashboard must be ready before Wave 1. Wave 3 (October) coincides with the upper primary pilot launch — onboarding 52 new secondary schools simultaneously with a new primary pilot is a significant resourcing risk that needs active management. Upper primary must have its own dedicated scoping track and team in Term 3.

What we need to deliver the plan
The team has the right skills and commitment. What we're asking for is capacity to match the ambition — three targeted hires that unlock the full roadmap. Without these, we will need to formally descope before Term 3 begins.
Ask 1
Project Manager
Role already approved — prioritise filling before Term 3 begins. To own the 132-school rollout operations — teacher onboarding, wave coordination, and school communications. This frees the PM to focus on product strategy rather than logistics.
Ask 2
Full-stack Engineer (full-time)
Brings the team to 2 full-time SWEs instead of the current 1.2 FTE — with flexibility to own both student-facing features and infrastructure work. The student-facing features that move the north star (continue where you left off, goal-to-session loop, habit mechanics, adaptive content) are not deliverable without this. A full-stack hire is more versatile than a front-end specialist and strengthens the team for scale.
Ask 3
UX Designer (shared resource with Student Space)
A shared UX Designer across LangBuddy and Student Space covers both user research and design — from understanding why students do not practise after school, to designing the student experience and teacher dashboards. One person who can do the research and translate it into product design, shared across two programmes to reduce cost. Without this, student features are designed without insight and teacher tools remain deprioritised.

The strategic gap this roadmap is designed to close
The pilot shows LangBuddy works as a teacher-initiated tool. Only 12.1% of students used it outside school hours with zero self-direction features built. Every feature priority is evaluated against closing that gap — while building the content infrastructure and safety layer needed for 132-school scale, and preparing for the upper primary expansion.

Student experience
Student
"Continue where you left off"
Returning students see their last topic, current milestone, and next session suggestion. Makes re-entry purposeful — directly targets the 440 students stuck at milestone 1.
Informed by Term 2 qual research. No visible next step is a likely driver of drop-off.
Student
Goal-to-session loop
Goals set at onboarding actively shape session content and visible progress. 923 students set goals — connect them to what happens next, not just logged once at onboarding.
Goals are the clearest intrinsic motivation signal in the pilot data. Closing this loop turns stated intention into action.
Teacher
Class assignment and scheduling
Teachers assign specific topics with due dates. Bridges structured curriculum and student-initiated use — a starting point that can grow into independent habit over time.
Structured sessions retain better (3.7 vs 2.7 avg sessions/student). Curriculum integration is the bridge to student-initiated practice.

Student experience — secondary
Student
Streak and progress visibility
Streak mechanic and milestone progress anchored to stated goals — purposeful, not gamified. Designed using Term 2 qual research on what actually motivates secondary MTL students to return.
Habit mechanics are the direct lever for moving avg sessions/week from 0.5 toward 3 — must reflect actual motivation drivers from qual research.
Student
Difficulty calibration
Auto-adjust session difficulty based on performance signals — response length, vocabulary range, turn count. Prevents frustration and boredom, which both suppress return.
Requires speaking progress data from Term 2 onwards. Cannot be built without that signal being stable.

Adaptive content layer
Content adapts to each student's level, progression, and gaps — vocabulary complexity, sentence scaffolding, topic depth. Moves from uniform to personalised practice at scale.
Requires knowledge base (Term 2) + content pipeline (Jun) + progress data (Term 2 onwards). All three must be stable first.
